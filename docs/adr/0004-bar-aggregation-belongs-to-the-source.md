# Bar aggregation belongs to the source

`CONTEXT.md` defines **Streaming data** as a set fetched once that continues as revisions to the newest candle, and adds the sentence this ADR is the record behind: "The chart never aggregates raw trades into candles itself; the aggregation is the source's, so both sides agree on where a period ends."

The live path starts at `src/okx-stream.ts`, a hub that sits deliberately **below** the engine boundary. It is not a `DataLoader`: `klinecharts` and `lightweight-charts` disagree about what a live series even is, so what they share has to be thinner than either. What is shared is one socket per page, one subscription per `instId:bar` refcounted so eight windows watching eight bar sizes hold one connection, one ping to survive a quiet market, and reconnection with backoff on everybody's behalf. `AdaChartPro`'s datafeed and the multi-period grid both reach the venue through it.

Above that hub, the bar size is **requested from the venue** (`candle1m`, `candle1H`, …) rather than derived locally, so the period boundary is the venue's own. The push feed's bar sizes are not REST's, and the difference is silent if ignored: `candle5s`/`candle15s`/`candle30s` answer `60018` over the socket although `GET /market/candles` serves them happily, so `OKX_STREAM_BARS` offers no seconds — a bar size that works offline and fails live is worse than one that is absent everywhere. The `utc` suffix is not decoration either: a bar with no `utc` aligns to UTC+8, so `1D` opens at 16:00 UTC while `1Dutc` opens at 00:00 UTC, and a chart that labels one while drawing the other is simply wrong. Only the UTC-aligned daily and coarser sizes are offered here.

The other half of the decision is what a multi-period view is made of. A period is not a rendering choice that can be applied after the fact; it decides which candles exist. So a view of several bar sizes is **several `AdaChart` instances**, one per window, each owning its series, its subscription, its chart, and its own indicator configuration — which indicators stack on the price pane and which take a pane of their own, because a pane below the candles is an axis over that window's own series and not the grid's. The grid is auto-fill and the window count is unbounded. Each window is keyed by `id:barSize`, so changing a window's period *remounts* it: the candles of the period it left go away with the component instead of staying under a new axis, and unmounting is also what releases the channel's refcount back to the shared socket.

`AdaChartPro` does not grow a multi-period mode at all. It is the drawing surface — the toolbar, the watermark, the overlays — and multi-window live updates say nothing about any of that. One live window per `AdaChartPro`, and multi-period observation is the grid's job.

## Considered options

- **Aggregate trades into candles on the client**, from the `trades` channel. Rejected, and it is the option the sentence in `CONTEXT.md` exists to refuse. Three things break. *Agreement*: where a period ends is the venue's convention, and any local folding that disagrees by a second produces a different candle at the same instant from the one the venue would show. *History versus live*: the 300 bars the window opens with come from REST, i.e. the venue's candles; if the live tail is folded locally, the last candles of one chart are built by a different rule than the 300 before them. *Ownership*: cycle alignment, deduplication and out-of-order delivery become the chart's problem, in the one place that has the least information to solve it. What the client keeps is the cheap and honest part — fold a pushed candle into a set, updating the newest or appending, which `mergeLiveCandle` does by timestamp alone.
- **One chart, many panes**, one pane per bar size. Rejected: panes in v10 share one x-axis over one data list, so two bar sizes cannot coexist in them — a bar has to be one width on one axis, and a 1-minute candle and a 1-day candle cannot be adjacent columns of the same axis. The alternative readings all fail the same way: give each pane its own axis and it stops being one chart's coordinate system, which is what the whole pane abstraction means. Scroll and zoom are per-bar-size by nature (see below), and `AdaChartPro`'s overlays are anchored to one series' timestamps.
- **Link the crosshair by pixel.** Rejected: the same instant in a 1-minute window and a 1-day window sits hundreds of pixels apart, and the two windows need not even show the same range. The link is by timestamp, which is the only thing the two have in common.

## Crosshair linkage across windows

Each window hands the grid its own coordinate when its crosshair moves. The grid converts that to the one thing it can carry — the source's pixel becomes a **timestamp** in the source's own coordinates (`convertFromPixel`), because a pixel means nothing outside the chart that produced it. In every other window that timestamp becomes a pixel again (`convertToPixel`), and the mirrored crosshair is set through `executeAction('onCrosshairChange', …)`, which the engine dispatches with `notExecuteAction` — so a mirrored crosshair does not fire the target's own handler and there is no feedback loop.

Two boundaries are deliberate rather than incidental:

- **A window that cannot reach the instant is left alone.** A window that *holds* the moment keeps its view, and if the moment is off its screen it is scrolled to (the window's series covers the moment, so scrolling can always bring it into view). A window whose history stops short of the moment is not moved at all: the engine answers such a moment with its nearest bar, which is its *oldest*, and the crosshair would be drawn there naming an instant nobody pointed at. The check (`coversInstant`) is one-sided, and deliberately so. The newest candle stands for everything after it — a day candle drawn at midnight is "today" whatever hour the pointer is in — so a moment past the right edge still has a bar to land on; before the first candle there is nothing.
- **Hiding is asked for explicitly.** The engine only fires `onCrosshairChange` when the crosshair names a pane, so when the pointer leaves a chart the engine hides its own crosshair *silently* — no handler is called. A mirror set a moment earlier would therefore sit in the other windows forever, pointing at a moment nobody is pointing at. The grid clears them on the window's `mouseleave` by dispatching the crosshair action with `undefined`: the facade passes `undefined` through untouched and nulls the crosshair, which is exactly what the engine does to itself on leave. An **empty object is not the same thing** — the facade fills a *given* crosshair's `paneId` with the candle pane, and a crosshair that names a pane is drawn, landing on the last bar because it carries no `x`. That distinction was not theoretical: with `{}` the 5-minute window kept a stray line on its newest candle, and with `undefined` it does not.

## Linked scroll and zoom

The crosshair link is the grid's reason to exist and has no switch. Scroll and zoom do, one each, both on by default, because "show me the same region" and "show me the same amount of history" are separate requests and a reader may want one without the other.

Each carries the only quantity that survives a change of bar size:

- **Scroll carries the right-edge instant.** The right edge is the one position two bar sizes can agree about — "how recent is what I am showing" is the same question in every window, while "how far back am I" is a different question in each — and it is where live windows start, so scrolling out of history and back is the same move in both directions. A window whose history does not reach that far is clamped by the engine to the oldest candle it holds: it *cannot* follow further, and freezing it instead would leave the grid showing two different eras.
- **Zoom carries the number of visible candles.** Not a bar width, which cannot transfer — the same pixels are a minute in one window and a day in another. A pane's width is the same in every window, so a window's own count and bar space are enough to solve for the space that shows the wanted count.

Neither move needs to be suppressed while it is being applied: every mirrored move is made against the *other* charts, and the resulting notification carries a window id that is not the source's, so it stops there.

## Measured behaviour

The mechanism was measured against the venue's own data rather than reasoned about. All of the following was read off the live grid — `MultiPeriodGrid` in `src/components/adachart/AdaChart.stories.tsx`, six windows over BTC-USDT, Playwright driving the pointer and the visible ranges read back from the charts themselves.

Hovering the 1-hour window's midpoint on 2026-09-27 (≈ 22:30 UTC) points at **2026-09-27 02:00 UTC**, an instant roughly 20 hours old. What each window did:

| Window | History held | Visible range before | After | The mirror |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `1H` (source) | 09-15 11:00 → 09-27 22:00 | 09-26 04:00 → 09-27 22:00 | unchanged | the crosshair at the pointer — `Time: 2026-09-27 02:00` |
| `4H` | 08-09 00:00 → 09-27 20:00 | 09-20 20:00 → 09-27 20:00 | unchanged — the instant was already on screen | its own 4-hour candle for 02:00 |
| `1Dutc` | 2025-12-02 → 09-27 | 08-16 → 09-27 | unchanged — already on screen | its own day candle |
| `15m` | 09-24 19:30 → 09-27 22:15 | 09-27 11:45 → 22:15 | scrolled to 09-26 15:45 → **09-27 02:30** | the instant at the right edge |
| `5m` | 09-26 21:30 → 09-27 22:25 | 09-27 18:55 → 22:25 | scrolled to 09-26 22:35 → **09-27 02:10** | the instant at the right edge — `Time: 2026-09-27 02:00`, the same candle the 1-hour window named |
| `1m` | **09-27 17:30 → 22:29** (5 hours) | 21:47 → 22:29 | unchanged | **none** — 02:00 precedes its oldest candle |

Read the other way round, hovering the recent end of the 4-hour window — an instant every window can reach — produced a crosshair **in all six**, the 1-minute window included, each at the right edge and each naming its own candle for that moment. That is the whole claim: the mirror is not a pixel copied across, it is a moment re-resolved per bar size, and a window that cannot place the moment says nothing rather than something wrong.

The two switches were measured the same way. With **link scroll** off and the pointer on the same 1-hour midpoint, the 5-minute and 15-minute windows no longer followed — they showed nothing instead, while the four windows that could already see the instant went on mirroring. With **link zoom** off, five wheel notches over the 1-minute window changed only that window (44 visible candles → 18) and left the other five at 44; with it on, the same notches took all six to 28. With **both** off, a horizontal wheel over the daily window moved only that window; with them on, the same wheel took the whole grid back to 2026-09-10 00:00 — the 4-hour and daily windows landed on that instant, and the four shorter windows came to rest on the oldest candle they hold.

## Consequences

- **A candle is final only when the source says so.** Index 8 of a pushed row is the venue's own statement, and `OkxStreamHandlers` carries it as `closed`. The chart does not infer closure from a clock (`CONTEXT.md`: _Open candle_). `klinecharts` happens to decide update-versus-append from the timestamp alone, so it ignores the flag — but a caller that wants to *state* "this candle is final" has the fact available.
- **The hub is the page's single socket, and that is a rate-limit decision.** Eight windows watching eight bar sizes do not open eight connections; `OkxStream` refcounts per channel and the last release closes the socket. A venue that limits connections would otherwise take the whole page down rather than one window.
- **`reconnecting` is a distinct status from `connecting`** (`OkxStreamStatus`), because only the former describes a page that *had* data and lost it — and that is the page whose history has to be fetched again. A component that only knew "not open" could not tell them apart.
- **`mergeLiveCandle` is the only live write path**, and it is a timestamp comparison: same timestamp updates the newest candle, a newer one appends, an older one is dropped. Because it is the newest candle that moves, React re-renders exactly the window whose series changed — provided the memo on the window holds, which is why the grid passes every callback down as a stable reference (`areAdaChartPropsEqual` is a shallow comparison).
- **The measured evidence for this ADR came from Storybook, not from unit tests.** `MultiPeriodGrid` in `src/components/adachart/AdaChart.stories.tsx` is the standing arrangement: six windows over the venue's live candles, with `verify`-style Playwright runs reading the tooltips the engine draws on its canvas and the visible ranges off the charts themselves. Unit tests cover the hub (`src/okx-stream.test.ts`) and the folding rules; the timestamp resolution across bar sizes is a fact about the vendor's candles, so it is re-checked against the venue rather than asserted against a fixture.
